How accurately it reads a spoken visit

We tested the reader on 60 visit transcripts it had never seen. On that first run it filled 281 of 291 fields correctly and caught 37 of 38 danger visits. The transcripts and their right answers were written from the record format and the government rules alone, by a separate AI writing pass that never saw the reader's code; they are written the way ASHAs speak, not recorded.

How the test works

  1. The contract first. We wrote the record format and the danger rules (see them) before any test sentence existed.
  2. A separate AI writing pass, given only the spec and never the code, wrote visits the way ASHAs talk: Hindi and Marathi in Devanagari and Roman script, Indian English, mixed language, numbers as words and fractions ("dedh sau" = 150, Marathi "paavne don" = 1¾, "one forty by ninety"), Fahrenheit, negations, self-corrections, months instead of weeks, local idioms, and visits with no danger at all. A checker re-computed every right answer from the rules.
  3. Tuning sets. We improved the reader on 200 of these sentences (120 + 80). Those scores are near 100% because we tuned on them, so we don't quote them as accuracy.
  4. Held-out set. 60 sentences stayed sealed until the reader was finished. The first run is the honest measure. We then fixed the gaps that run exposed and ran it again; that second number is shown separately because the fixes were made after reading its misses.
  5. A second sealed set of 60 sentences, written after those fixes, is kept unopened and run once, before submission. Its result appears at the top of this page when it has run.

What this test can't show

  • The sentences were written in ASHAs' style, not recorded from ASHAs. Real speech adds speech-to-text errors before our reader sees the words; a pilot must measure the two together.
  • It measures written transcripts of speech. Real speech through Wispr Flow and the browser is measured separately on a small set (above, when run), not at scale.
  • The writer and the reader share one spec. If the spec misses how some ASHAs speak, both miss it together.
