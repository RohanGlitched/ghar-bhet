The problem is real, and it happens every day

An ASHA is the health system's first contact for every pregnancy and newborn in her village. After each home visit she fills a register, a diary and, more and more, government apps.

  • 10.29 lakh ASHAs serve India's villages (PIB, January 2025). About 70,000 work in Maharashtra.
  • Around 1.5 crore newborns a year get ASHA home visits under the newborn programme (SSBSK guidelines, 2026).
  • Records are kept twice. ASHAs in Maharashtra describe entering the same visit on paper and again in apps, late into the night (reported by New Lines Magazine).
  • Typing is the barrier. In a 2024 study, ASHAs found typing Hindi hard and used voice dictation and voice notes instead (ASHABot, arXiv 2409.10913).
  • Danger signs are easy to miss on paper. A baby under 1800 g, breathing over 60 a minute, or a mother with BP over 140/90 needs a referral the same day.

Why voice makes it better

The organisers asked: "Is the problem real and frequent, and does voice make it better?" For an ASHA, voice isn't a feature on top. It is how she already reports to the ANM.

Her hands are busy

She is holding a baby, a thermometer or a weighing sling. Speaking needs no hands and no screen.

She speaks, she doesn't type

She speaks Hindi or Marathi with numbers as words: "dedh sau", "saadhe aath", "ek kilo saat sau gram". Ghar Bhet reads all of these.

Danger is said out loud

When a rule fires, the phone says it in her language: "Refer today." She hears it in the home, not later at her desk.

How one visit works

One narrow flow, end to end, done by speaking: a home visit becomes a checked record and, if needed, a referral the nurse sees at once.

  1. She speaks the visit

    "Sunita ka baby, saat din ka, wajan ek kilo saat sau gram, doodh kam pee raha, pasli chal rahi hai."

  2. The record fills itself

    Day 7, 1700 g, feeding less, chest indrawing. Every value is marked in the words it came from.

  3. Fixed rules check it

    Weight under 1800 g and chest indrawing are danger signs under the 2026 newborn guidelines. REFER TODAY.

  4. It asks what's missing

    "What is the baby's temperature?" She answers by voice, or corrects herself: "nahi nahi, 37.8".

  5. The nurse sees it live

    One tap saves it. The ANM's board shows the open referral and a printable slip.

Danger signs come from fixed, cited rules

A model never decides who is in danger. Each flag comes from a rule in the government manuals and names the clause it comes from, so an ANM can check every decision.

Measured on speech it had never seen, misses included

We wrote the rules and the record format first. Then a separate writer, who never saw the code, wrote spoken visits and the right answers. We tuned on 200 of them and kept 60 sealed until the end.

96.6%of fields right, held-out first run (281 of 291)
98.6%of filled fields were correct (precision)
37 of 38danger visits flagged; 1 missed on the first run
1 of 22normal visits wrongly flagged on the first run

The first run missed one danger visit, a Marathi way of saying "the baby moves only when touched", and raised one false alarm, from a self-correction ("five pads… no, four"). We fixed both and re-ran: 99.3% of fields and 38 of 38 danger visits. Those fixes were made after reading the held-out misses, so the first run is the honest number. Every result and every miss.

What it depends on, and what happens if that stops

The reader and the rules are our own code: about 1,060 lines of plain JavaScript, with no dependencies. There is no AI service to pay for or lose.

PartUses todayIf it gets costly or stops
Hearing her voiceThe browser's own speech recognition (Chrome and Edge, Hindi, Marathi, English)Typing always works. Any speech engine that returns text can be swapped in.

What it is not, yet

Honest limits, so nobody has to guess.

  • Not a medical device. The rules support the ANM's and doctor's judgement; they never replace it.
  • Not yet tested with ASHAs. The test sentences were written in the style ASHAs speak, not recorded from ASHAs. The next step is a pilot with ASHAs in one Pune block.
  • Speech-to-text depends on the browser. Chrome and Edge listen in Hindi, Marathi and English; other browsers can type.
  • The village on the board is a sample. The people are made up; visits you speak are added live.
