Speak a home visit

You are the village health worker (ASHA). Tap the mic and say the visit.

How to speak
  • Pick who you visited and your language, tap the mic, and say what you found, the way you would tell the nurse: name, age, weight, temperature, breathing, feeding, any danger sign.
  • The phone asks for what is missing, out loud, and opens the mic for your answer. "Haan" and "nahi" work, and so do "chhodo" (skip) and "pata nahi" (not checked).
  • Say "save karo" to save: the phone reads the record back and saves on "haan". "Padh ke sunao" reads it back; "bas" stops the mic.
  • Works in Chrome or Edge. Anywhere else, or with no internet, type it below or use your keyboard's mic, or try an example.

How it understands you
  1. Speak in your own words. Hindi, Marathi or English, numbers as digits or words: "dedh sau" (150), "saadhe aath" (8.5), "ek kilo saat sau gram" (1.7 kg).
  2. Every value keeps the words it came from: tap a value to light them up. If nothing matches, the field stays empty and the phone asks for it.
  3. The phone asks for what is missing, out loud, and listens for the answer. "Haan" and "nahi" work, and so do "chhodo" (skip) and "pata nahi" (not checked).
  4. Danger signs come from fixed rules, the ones in the Health Ministry (MoHFW) manuals and the 2026 newborn guidelines, never from a model.
  5. Correct it by voice. "Nahi nahi, 150 by 100": the last value said wins.
  6. Save by voice. Say "save karo"; the phone reads the record back and saves when you say "haan".
